# Practical paths to launch and sustain a public "AI scenario map": funders, hosts, partners, credibility, MVP

Research date: 9 October 2026. Many sources below are from 2024 and 2025. Figures that come only from third-party wikis (mainly LongtermWiki) or grant aggregators are marked "secondary" and should be checked against the primary site before being quoted publicly. Format note: each finding ends with its source in parentheses.

---

## 1. Comparable public-interest AI projects: how they began, team size, first version, funding

### Takeaway
Almost every comparable project started as a small volunteer or side effort (1 to 7 people) with a narrow first version, then got a fiscal sponsor or a host institution, then one large anchor grant, usually from Open Philanthropy (now Coefficient Giving) or the Survival and Flourishing Fund. The projects that struggled (Stampy/aisafety.info) are the ones that never found steady funding for maintenance, which is the main long-term risk for a living map.

### Cited Findings

**AI Incident Database (AIID), the closest structural analogue (curated public database plus submissions)**
- Launched publicly in November 2020 under the Partnership on AI, a nonprofit consortium of companies and institutions. ([The Batch, DeepLearning.AI](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/cataloging-ai-gone-wrong))
- First version was small: 1,174 unique articles covering 77 incidents at launch, per project lead Sean McGregor. ([The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/cataloging-ai-gone-wrong))
- Intake model from day one: the public submits incidents based on media reports, and editors decide whether a report is a new incident or added to an existing one. ([The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/cataloging-ai-gone-wrong))
- The first formal taxonomy came later (July 2021) and was built in collaboration with Georgetown's Center for Security and Emerging Technology (CSET). ([AIID blog](https://incidentdatabase.ai/blog/the-first-taxonomy-of-ai-incidents))
- McGregor published an academic paper on it at AAAI 2021, describing 1,000+ incident reports. ([AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/17817))
- Funding grew fivefold, which was used to create a standalone nonprofit, the Responsible AI Collaborative, which began hiring founding staff in 2022. ([AIID blog, Join RAIC](https://incidentdatabase.ai/blog/join-raic))
- A grant directory lists the Collaborative's IRS ruling as 2023 and says expenses run through fiscal sponsor Players Philanthropy Fund (secondary). ([GrantedAI](https://grantedai.com/foundations/responsible-ai-collaborative))
- By January 2026: 5,000+ manually annotated incident reports, 400,000+ active users in 2025, and nearly 80,000 full-database downloads in one month. Hosting costs spiked when traffic outgrew free scaling. ([AIID blog, "Funding the AIID part 1", 25 Jan 2026](https://incidentdatabase.ai/blog/funding-the-aiid-part-1/))
- Current funding model: individual donations, a one-year corporate sponsorship trial led by Trustible.ai (sponsors get early data access and "direct input"), and philanthropy. The project says philanthropic grants are poorly suited to ongoing maintenance. ([AIID blog](https://incidentdatabase.ai/blog/funding-the-aiid-part-1/))

**MIT AI Risk Repository (closest content analogue: a "finite map" of risks with a taxonomy)**
- Released 14 August 2024 by MIT FutureTech with MIT CSAIL and outside partners (University of Queensland, Future of Life Institute, KU Leuven, Harmony Intelligence). Leads: Peter Slattery, Neil Thompson, Alexander Saeri, Emily Grundy, plus five collaborators. ([MIT FutureTech](https://futuretech.mit.edu/news/presenting-the-ai-risk-repository))
- First version: a "living database" of 700+ risks, extracted from a review of 43 existing frameworks, sorted by a 7-domain / 23-subdomain taxonomy and a causal taxonomy. Headline finding: the most thorough single framework missed about 30% of risks found across all frameworks. ([MIT FutureTech](https://futuretech.mit.edu/news/presenting-the-ai-risk-repository); [BigDATAwire](https://bigdatawire.com/2024/08/15/mit-releases-a-comprehensive-repository-of-ai-risks); [CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/3487207/mit-delivers-database-containing-700-risks-associated-with-ai.html))
- Later versions reportedly in December 2024 and April 2025, reaching 1,700+ risks by December 2025 (secondary). ([LongtermWiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/knowledge-base/responses/epistemic-tools/tools/mit-ai-risk-repository))

**AI Futures Project / AI 2027 (closest analogue for a scenario product)**
- Small research nonprofit; its first project, AI 2027, took the team "more than half a year" and launched as a single detailed scenario website in April 2025. ([AI Futures blog](https://blog.ai-futures.org/p/our-first-project-ai-2027); [ai-2027.com/about](https://ai-2027.com/about))
- Team of about five named people: Daniel Kokotajlo (executive director, ex-OpenAI governance researcher), Eli Lifland, Thomas Larsen, Romeo Dean, Jonas Vollmer (COO). ([ai-2027.com/about](https://ai-2027.com/about))
- Funding as of September 2025: $1.44M from the Survival and Flourishing Fund plus $3.05M from private donors, with SFF matching the next $500K of donations. Raised via a public Manifund page. ([Manifund](https://manifund.org/projects/ai-forecasting-and-policy-research-by-the-ai-2027-team)) Possibly stale (over a year old).

**Epoch AI (data/trends org)**
- Founded April 2022 by Jaime Sevilla and six co-founders, growing out of a volunteer effort to study machine learning trends. ([Epoch 2022 impact report](https://epoch.ai/blog/epoch-impact-report-2022))
- First year: $1.96M from Open Philanthropy; fiscally sponsored and operationally supported by Rethink Priorities' Special Projects team; 13 staff (about 9 to 11 full-time equivalents) by early 2023. ([Epoch 2022 impact report](https://epoch.ai/blog/epoch-impact-report-2022))
- Reportedly spun out as an independent 501(c)(3) in early 2025 (secondary). ([LongtermWiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/wiki/epoch-ai))

**AI Impacts (expert surveys on AI timelines and risk)**
- Co-founded 2014 by Katja Grace and Paul Christiano. ([AI Impacts about](https://aiimpacts.org/about/); [LongtermWiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/wiki/ai-impacts))
- Open Philanthropy granted $345,000 in August 2023 for its expert survey on progress in AI. ([Good Ventures grant record](https://www.goodventures.org/?p=3876))
- The 2023 survey reached 2,778 AI researchers. ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/RkegCmCgjGhskiFvm/ai-impacts-2023-expert-survey-on-progress-in-ai)) Its survey probabilities are a likely input for the scenario map.

**Our World in Data (OWID) AI pages**
- The AI topic page was expanded on 21 September 2023 with 23 new charts by Charlie Giattino, Edouard Mathieu, Veronika Samborska and Max Roser. ([OWID blog](https://ourworldindata.org/blog/page/5))
- OWID is a collaboration between University of Oxford researchers and the nonprofit Global Change Data Lab, which publishes the site. ([OWID motivation](https://ourworldindata.org/motivation))
- Reported $3M over three years in general support from Open Philanthropy, July 2025 (secondary). ([LongtermWiki grant record](https://www.longtermwiki.com/grants/GKPcTOfSbR))

**Stampy's AI Safety Info (aisafety.info), a cautionary example**
- Began September 2020 as a Discord bot in Rob Miles's AI safety community (secondary). ([LongtermWiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/wiki/E382))
- Built by volunteers, with a paid three-month writing fellowship at one point. ([EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/4cCRCoYvcLEr7prC2/ai-safety-info-distillation-fellowship); [soft launch post](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/mHNoaNvpEuzzBEEfg/stampy-s-ai-safety-info-soft-launch))
- Funding trouble: a Manifund application shows as not funded, and an Every.org campaign states a normal burn rate of about $12k/month cut to $6k on a "skeleton crew." ([Manifund](https://manifund.org/projects/stampys-ai-safety-info); [Every.org](https://www.every.org/aisafetyinfo/f/keep-stampy-alive))

**The Midas Project (one-person-started watchdog)**
- Founded and led by Tyler Johnston; 501(c)(3). ([Manifund](https://manifund.org/projects/the-midas-project); [Every.org](https://www.every.org/the-midas-project))
- Early funding post: operated on under $25,000 plus volunteers; largest institutional grant so far $31k from SFF; sought $119k, with $30k enough for a second hire. ([Manifund](https://manifund.org/projects/the-midas-project)) Likely stale.

**Future of Life Institute (FLI) AI Safety Index (a rating product, analogous for credibility design)**
- Uses a volunteer independent review panel of academics (chosen from academia to reduce conflicts of interest) who grade companies on 42 indicators (2024 edition). ([FLI AI Safety Index 2024](https://futureoflife.org/document/fli-ai-safety-index-2024/))
- 2024 panel reportedly included Yoshua Bengio, Stuart Russell, David Krueger, Jessica Newman, Atoosa Kasirzadeh, Tegan Maharaj, Sneha Revanur (secondary). ([AI Governance Library](https://www.aigl.blog/fli-ai-safety-index-2024/))
- July 2026 edition: seven outside reviewers, 37 indicators in six categories, based on public documents plus a company survey; five of nine companies answered the survey. ([Axios, 7 Jul 2026](https://axios.com/2026/07/07/report-ai-safety-pledges))

**Collective Intelligence Project (CIP) Global Dialogues (public-input analogue)**
- Monthly public-input process run with Remesh (deliberation) and Prolific (participant sampling). ([CIP Global Dialogues](https://www.cip.org/global-dialogues))
- First pilot engaged 1,200 participants in 68 countries and 8 languages, and was described as 10 to 100 times cheaper than comparable efforts. ([CIP 2024 Annual Report](https://2024.cip.org/2024_Annual_Report_CIP.pdf))
- CIP says it is funded only by philanthropic donations and does not accept commercial partnership money. ([CIP funding partnerships](https://cip.org/funding-partnerships))

**Forecasting Research Institute (FRI) Existential Risk Persuasion Tournament (XPT), a model for presenting "both camps"**
- 169 participants (89 superforecasters, 80 domain experts) forecast in June to October 2022, then debated and updated. ([Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/robots-ai/ai-predictions-superforecasters?amp=1))
- AI produced the biggest gap: experts' median 3% chance of AI-caused extinction by 2100 vs 0.38% for superforecasters; 12% vs 2.13% for a catastrophe killing 10%+ of humans. ([Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/robots-ai/ai-predictions-superforecasters?amp=1))
- Debate produced little convergence (secondary). ([LongtermWiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/wiki/xpt))

### Inferences
- The proven launch pattern is: narrow first version, public submissions with an editor in the loop, a methodology paper or post, then a partner institution for a taxonomy or review. AIID launched with only 77 incidents and that was enough.
- AI 2027 shows that a single well-made scenario website from a five-person team can get global attention; a map of 10 to 20 scenarios from one person is a smaller but similar shape.
- Maintenance funding, not launch funding, is where small projects fail (AIID says so directly; Stampy is the example). Plan the MVP so one person can maintain it in a few hours a month.

### Gaps
- Could not find the founding budget or team size for Tech Policy Press, or details on Pol.is deployments as a host model. Not researched for time.
- No primary source found for the AIID's early Partnership on AI funding amounts.
- MIT AI Risk Repository funding source is not stated on the launch page.
- AI Impacts' 2025 to 2026 operating status could not be confirmed.

## 2. Funders active in 2025 to 2026, safety-focused vs balanced/civic, and typical sizes

### Takeaway
The money for AI-future work splits into two pools. The large, fast, individual-friendly pool (Coefficient Giving, SFF, Long-Term Future Fund, Manifund, FLI) is safety-focused and associated with effective altruism, which is the camp the "accelerationist" side distrusts. The civic pool (Humanity AI coalition of MacArthur, Omidyar, Mozilla and others; Knight; OpenAI's People-First AI Fund; Anthropic's Economic Futures) is framed around people, democracy and the economy, and is slower and more oriented to established nonprofits. A balanced map may need money from both pools to look balanced.

### Cited Findings

**Safety-focused pool**
- **Coefficient Giving (formerly Open Philanthropy):** renamed in November 2025; moved from serving mainly Good Ventures (Dustin Moskovitz and Cari Tuna) to 13 cause-specific funds open to multiple donors (secondary). ([LongtermWiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/knowledge-base/organizations/funders/coefficient-giving))
- Its AI Governance request for proposals closed 25 January 2026; it says it will likely open more but has not decided when. One listing says grants typically run $200k to $2M a year, but that listing notes its text was AI-generated, so treat as unverified. ([EA Opportunities listing](https://www.effectivealtruism.org/opportunities/recTVSiPgDoRHKJrz))
- A capacity-building open call (training, events, groups, "resources" addressing risks from advanced AI) was circulated 20 March 2026; size and current status unknown. ([UCSC research toolkit notice](https://toolkit.science.ucsc.edu/?p=12463))
- Coefficient/Open Phil has funded the forecasting infrastructure a scenario map would draw on: $2.75M to Metaculus for platform development (April 2024) and $532,400 for LLM-vs-human forecasting tournaments. ([Open Philanthropy grant page](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/grants/metaculus-platform-development-2024); [tournaments grant](https://openphilanthropy.org/grants/metaculus-forecasting-tournaments))
- **Survival and Flourishing Fund (SFF), funded mainly by Jaan Tallinn:** 2025 round reportedly about $34.33M to about 89 organizations, roughly 86% to AI safety and governance; median grant around $100K (secondary). ([LongtermWiki SFF](https://www.longtermwiki.com/wiki/sff))
- SFF's 2025 round used Main, Freedom and Fairness tracks with 12 recommenders. ([LongtermWiki SFF](https://www.longtermwiki.com/knowledge-base/organizations/funders/sff)) The "Freedom" and "Fairness" tracks are notable for a balanced project.
- SFF funded small, newly founded projects: $31k to the Midas Project early on, $1.44M to AI Futures Project. ([Manifund, Midas](https://manifund.org/projects/the-midas-project); [Manifund, AI Futures](https://manifund.org/projects/ai-forecasting-and-policy-research-by-the-ai-2027-team))
- **Long-Term Future Fund (EA Funds):** March 2022 to March 2023 funded 263 of 878 applications, about $9.1M total, average $34.6k per grant; acceptance rate 19.3% in 2023 to 2024. ([EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/nLxpFeEs6kAdgjRWz)) Median reportedly about $25K (secondary). ([LongtermWiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/resources/9baa7f54db71864d)) Funds individuals, which suits a solo founder.
- **Manifund:** regranting platform where expert regrantors with budgets fund projects; Manifund handles fiscal sponsorship, grant agreements and payouts, and can pay individuals; grants and reasoning are public. ([Manifund regranting](https://dev.manifund.org/about/regranting); [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/MFaHQN38KBZDpdzrX/why-self-fund-your-regranting)) Used by AI Futures Project, Midas Project and Stampy to raise publicly.
- **Future of Life Institute:** issues time-limited requests for proposals and contests rather than open applications; a recent call (religious projects on the AGI race, deadline 2 Feb 2026) offered $30,000 to $300,000. ([FLI 2025 grants](https://futureoflife.org/grant-program/2025-grants/); [EA Opportunities listing](https://www.effectivealtruism.org/opportunities/recHwrs1MDsg3bioo)) FLI has also run a worldbuilding competition, which is close in spirit to a scenario map. ([FLI 2025 grants](https://futureoflife.org/grant-program/2025-grants/))
- **Schmidt Sciences:** AI Safety Science program awarded about $10M to 27 technical research projects (Feb 2025); a 2026 multi-agent safety call offered up to $300k or $1M, deadline 8 Aug 2026. ([Schmidt Sciences](https://www.schmidtsciences.org/new-10-million-ai-safety-science-program-launched-for-foundational-research/); [University at Albany notice](https://www.albany.edu/node/126771)) Academic and technical; a poor fit for a public website.

**Balanced / civic pool**
- **Humanity AI:** $500M five-year coalition launched 14 October 2025, co-chaired by MacArthur and Omidyar Network, with Mellon, Packard, Lumina, Kapor, Doris Duke, Mozilla, Siegel and Ford. Priorities include democracy, education, humanities, labor and security; pooled fund fiscally sponsored by Rockefeller Philanthropy Advisors; direct awards expected in 2026. ([Mellon Foundation](https://www.mellon.org/news/humanity-ai-commits-500m-build-people-centered-future-ai); [Anchorage Daily News / AP](https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2025/10/14/foundations-want-to-curb-ai-developers-influence-with-500-million-aimed-at-centering-human-needs/))
- An aggregator reports about $18M announced so far, with a $10M open call planned for summer 2026 (unverified). ([GrantedAI](https://grantedai.com/blog/macarthur-500-million-humanity-ai))
- The coalition's stated framing is to curb AI developers' influence, which positions it on one side of some debates. ([ADN / AP headline](https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2025/10/14/foundations-want-to-curb-ai-developers-influence-with-500-million-aimed-at-centering-human-needs/))
- **Omidyar Network** separately launched a $30M commitment in March 2025 for generative AI in the public interest, including a tech journalism fund. ([Omidyar](https://omidyar.com/?p=30473))
- **Knight Foundation:** 2025 AI activity found was small and research-oriented (up to $20,000 grants via Authors Alliance on AI, copyright and the public interest). ([Prime Progress listing](https://primeprogressng.com/opportunities/call-for-proposals-authors-alliance-offers-20k-for-ai-copyright-research/))
- **OpenAI People-First AI Fund:** $40.5M in unrestricted grants to 208 US nonprofits (from nearly 3,000 applicants) announced December 2025, plus $9.5M board-directed; four-question application open 8 Sep to 8 Oct 2025. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/my-MM/index/people-first-ai-fund-grantees/); [Gend summary](https://www.gend.co/blog/open-ai-people-first-ai-fund)) Aimed at community groups, AI literacy and civic life; requires a 501(c)(3).
- **Anthropic Economic Futures Program:** launched 27 June 2025 with rapid grants up to $50,000 plus $5,000 in API credits, for empirical research on AI's economic impacts, completable in under six months. ([Anthropic](https://anthropic.com/news/introducing-the-anthropic-economic-futures-program)) Economics-focused; only fits the jobs/economy scenarios.

### Inferences
- For a solo, non-501(c)(3) founder, the realistic first money is: self-funding, a Manifund page, an LTFF application (tens of thousands), or an SFF speculation grant. All are safety-leaning, which matters for perceived neutrality (see section 4).
- Civic funders (Humanity AI members, Knight, OpenAI's fund) mostly grant to established 501(c)(3)s. Reaching them likely requires a fiscal sponsor or a host institution first.
- AI company money (OpenAI, Anthropic) would be a credibility liability for a map that rates scenarios involving those companies, even if available.
- Breakthrough Energy was not researched; no evidence found that it funds AI-futures communication.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm Coefficient Giving's 2025 to 2026 AI spending totals from its own site. A figure circulating in search results ($168M in 2024, $351M in 2025, over $1B planned for AI policy advocacy in 2026, attributed to Coefficient Giving itself) could not be tied to a primary URL.
- Mozilla Foundation and Patrick J. McGovern Foundation current open calls not researched.
- No current (late 2026) open-call status confirmed for any funder above; all deadlines found have passed.

## 3. Partners or hosts likely to be seen as neutral by both camps

### Takeaway
No candidate host is cleanly neutral. Forecasting platforms (Metaculus, FRI) are the best fit for the "probabilities" layer and the XPT is a ready-made "both camps in numbers" source, but they are funded largely by Open Philanthropy/Coefficient Giving. Universities give the strongest neutral signal (MIT hosted the AI Risk Repository; Georgetown CSET co-built AIID's taxonomy), but they move slowly and want an academic lead.

### Cited Findings
- Metaculus says it works with nonprofits in its focus areas, including AI progress, and offers hosted forecasting tournaments, research collaboration, paid Pro Forecasters, and private forecasting spaces; it invites nonprofits to co-apply for grants. ([Metaculus](https://metaculus.com:443))
- Metaculus is heavily Open Philanthropy funded ($2.75M platform grant 2024, plus tournament grants) and separately announced a $5.5M grant to advance "forecasting as a public good." ([Open Philanthropy](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/grants/metaculus-platform-development-2024); [Metaculus Medium](https://metaculus.medium.com/metaculus-awarded-5-5m-grant-to-advance-forecasting-as-a-public-good-7fc20a161723))
- AIID's credibility step-up came from a university think tank partnership (Georgetown CSET) for its taxonomy. ([AIID blog](https://incidentdatabase.ai/blog/the-first-taxonomy-of-ai-incidents))
- The MIT AI Risk Repository was a university-hosted multi-institution effort that included the Future of Life Institute as a partner. ([MIT FutureTech](https://futuretech.mit.edu/news/presenting-the-ai-risk-repository))
- Politico reported in 2023 that Open Philanthropy had given tens of millions to AI researchers at RAND, Georgetown CSET, and the Center for a New American Security; critics argued this shapes Washington AI policy toward long-term risk. ([Politico via Yahoo syndication](https://news.yahoo.com/billionaire-backed-network-ai-advisers-090000786.html?fr=sycsrp_catchall); [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/10/20/2023/the-new-think-tanks-influencing-ai-policy-in-washington))
- CIP runs public-input processes with partner questions from labs, AI companies, civil society and governments, and refuses commercial funding. ([CIP Global Dialogues](https://www.cip.org/global-dialogues); [CIP funding](https://cip.org/funding-partnerships))

### Inferences
- RAND and CSET carry a "safety camp" association with some critics because of the Politico reporting, despite strong general reputations.
- The most practical partnership for an individual is a data partnership rather than a host: link to (or embed) live Metaculus questions and cite the XPT and AI Impacts surveys for probabilities, and ask CIP if Global Dialogues data on public views can be cited. This gets neutral-looking numbers without needing anyone's institutional approval.
- A university lab partnership (for example a methods review or taxonomy co-design, as CSET did for AIID) is a good second-year goal once the MVP exists.

### Gaps
- Did not research Stanford HAI, Brookings, or news-organization partnerships directly; no sourced evidence on whether they partner with individual-led projects.
- FRI's funding sources were not confirmed in this pass.

## 4. Credibility risks for an individual founder and how comparable projects handled them

### Takeaway
The biggest risk is being read as a front for one camp, mainly through funding source. Comparable projects manage this with an outside expert panel (FLI index), public submission plus editorial rules (AIID), published methodology and public grant reasoning (Manifund), and refusing certain money (CIP refuses commercial funding). FLI's own panel was all-academic and safety-leaning, which shows that "independent" is not the same as "balanced across camps."

### Cited Findings
- Effective altruism funding of AI policy work has drawn public criticism (Politico, Semafor), including calls for full disclosure of who funds fellows placed in Congress. ([Politico via Yahoo](https://news.yahoo.com/billionaire-backed-network-ai-advisers-090000786.html?fr=sycsrp_catchall); [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/10/20/2023/the-new-think-tanks-influencing-ai-policy-in-washington))
- Prominent skeptics dismiss doom scenarios outright (Yann LeCun: "These doomsday scenarios are absurd"), so a map that gives doom scenarios equal space will be attacked from that side too. ([Fox News](https://foxnews.com/politics/facebook-cofounder-dustin-moskovitz-effective-altruism-network-tied-ai-regulation-push))
- FLI chose its index panel from academia specifically to reduce conflicts of interest, and published grading methods and per-company justifications. ([FLI AI Safety Index 2024](https://futureoflife.org/document/fli-ai-safety-index-2024/))
- A review of FLI's 2024 index criticized it for not defining thresholds or testing details, showing that method transparency gets scrutinized. ([AI Governance Library](https://www.aigl.blog/fli-ai-safety-index-2024/))
- CIP's policy: philanthropic donations only, no commercial partnership money. ([CIP funding](https://cip.org/funding-partnerships))
- AIID's corporate sponsor trial gives sponsors "direct input into the database," a model that a scenario map should avoid or fence off. ([AIID blog](https://incidentdatabase.ai/blog/funding-the-aiid-part-1/))
- XPT organizers explicitly declined to declare which group (experts or superforecasters) was right, presenting both. ([Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/robots-ai/ai-predictions-superforecasters?amp=1))

### Inferences
- Practical safeguards for this project: a public funding page listing every donor over a set amount; an advisory group of 4 to 6 people with named members from both the safety and the acceleration/open-source camps (an all-academic panel is not enough); a rule that each scenario's case is written in quotes from its own proponents and that proponents can request corrections; and a published methodology page.
- The founder's lack of AI credentials is less of a problem if the map quotes others rather than arguing. AIID and the MIT repository both earned trust by curating, not opining.
- Accepting only one camp's funding early is survivable if disclosed, but a second funder from the other pool should be a stated goal.

### Gaps
- No sourced example found of an AI project that recruited advisors explicitly from both the safety and accelerationist camps; this would be new.
- No sourced data on how much funding disclosure actually changes reader trust.

## 5. What a minimum viable version could look like, and projects that launched small and grew

### Takeaway
Comparable projects launched with much less than a "complete" map: AIID with 77 incidents, AI 2027 with one scenario, CIP's pilot with 1,200 participants. An MVP of 10 to 20 scenarios on a static site, each with sourced quotes, any available probabilities, and a correction form, matches what worked elsewhere. Growth came from a methods paper, a partner for the taxonomy, and regular versioned updates.

### Cited Findings
- AIID: launched November 2020 with 77 incidents and a public submission form plus editor review; grew to 5,000+ reports and 400,000+ annual users by 2025. ([The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/cataloging-ai-gone-wrong); [AIID blog](https://incidentdatabase.ai/blog/funding-the-aiid-part-1/))
- MIT AI Risk Repository: launched as a "living database" built from a literature review, then versioned updates (700+ to 1,700+ risks). ([MIT FutureTech](https://futuretech.mit.edu/news/presenting-the-ai-risk-repository); [LongtermWiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/knowledge-base/responses/epistemic-tools/tools/mit-ai-risk-repository))
- AI 2027: one detailed scenario on a dedicated website, built over six-plus months by about five people. ([AI Futures blog](https://blog.ai-futures.org/p/our-first-project-ai-2027))
- Epoch: started as a volunteer collaboration around a single compute-trends paper before becoming an institute. ([Epoch 2022 impact report](https://epoch.ai/blog/epoch-impact-report-2022))
- CIP Global Dialogues: first pilot 1,200 participants, run with existing tools (Remesh, Prolific) rather than custom software. ([CIP 2024 Annual Report](https://2024.cip.org/2024_Annual_Report_CIP.pdf))
- AIID warns that traffic growth can push hosting past free tiers. ([AIID blog](https://incidentdatabase.ai/blog/funding-the-aiid-part-1/))

### Inferences (suggested MVP, not sourced)
- **Content:** 12 to 20 scenarios grouped into a small taxonomy (for example: who controls AI, how fast, economic outcome, safety outcome). Each scenario card: one-paragraph description, "in their own words" quotes from 2 to 3 named proponents with links, the strongest counter-argument quoted from named critics, probabilities where any exist (Metaculus question links, XPT, AI Impacts survey), and a policy-vs-communication triage tag.
- **Build:** static Next.js site with content in Markdown or a Supabase table, plus a correction/submission form with editor review (the AIID model). This is within the founder's existing skills.
