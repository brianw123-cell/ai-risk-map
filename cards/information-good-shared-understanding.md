# Better shared understanding and forecasting

**In one sentence:** AI helps people check facts, correct false beliefs, find common ground across disagreements and forecast events more accurately, so that societies argue from a more shared and more accurate picture of the world.

**Design note:** for a good-direction card, "worried" means worried this won't happen or is oversold, not worried that it will. This card bundles four strands that move at different speeds: correcting false beliefs, helping groups deliberate, crowd fact-checking, and forecasting (including weather). The evidence is strongest for weather and weakest for society-wide effects.

## Preconditions
- AI assistants become accurate and well-sourced enough on news and contested facts that relying on them improves people's beliefs rather than adding new errors.
- The systems people actually use are tuned to tell the truth, not to agree with the user or maximize engagement.
- Platforms adopt tools that reward agreement across divides (often called "bridging" algorithms: content is promoted only when people who usually disagree both rate it helpful) and keep them hard to game.
- People gain lasting judgment from these tools, not just better answers while the tool is switched on.
- AI forecasts are published openly and scored against what actually happens, so trust can be earned.

## Timing and reversibility
- **Earliest plausible date:** parts are already here. Machine-learning weather forecasts became part of a major operational forecast service in [February 2025](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/news/2025/ecmwfs-ai-forecasts-become-operational). The forecasting research group FRI reported in [July 2026](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-models-have-likely-reached-parity) that the best AI systems are now statistically indistinguishable from top human forecasters on its benchmark. A society-wide improvement in shared understanding has no date anyone has published.
- **Speed:** gradual. Each strand depends on platform choices, product design and slow changes in habits.
- **Reversibility:** not applicable for a benefit. The relevant risk is that the gains are outweighed by the same tools making things worse (see the opposite scenario), or that companies tune assistants toward flattery because flattery sells.

## The case that this happens (optimistic)
*Short excerpts for orientation only. A person from this side will choose the final quotes at sign-off.*

- Gordon Pennycook, a co-author of the [2024 Science study](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adq1814) in which conversations with GPT-4 Turbo cut belief in conspiracy theories by about 20% for at least two months, said the result shows ["evidence matters much more than we thought it did"](https://news.cornell.edu/node/329310) (Cornell, September 2024). His co-author David Rand said such models have ["the potential to counter conspiracies at a massive scale"](https://news.cornell.edu/node/329310).
- Michael Henry Tessler, Christopher Summerfield and colleagues at Google DeepMind reported in [Science in October 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adq2852) that an AI mediator (the "Habermas Machine") wrote group statements that 5,734 UK participants preferred to those written by human mediators, and that groups moved closer together after using it while minority views were still reflected. Audrey Tang, Taiwan's former digital minister, has long argued for [bridging tools that surface "uncommon ground"](https://rightlivelihood.org/the-change-makers/find-a-laureate/audrey-tang/) rather than amplifying the loudest voices.

**Plain summary (needs sign-off from this side):** For the first time there is controlled evidence that patient, personalized, fact-based conversation can change entrenched false beliefs, and that it works because of the facts, not because people trust machines. AI mediators can help groups find statements they genuinely share. Crowd fact-checking that requires agreement across political lines measurably slows the spread of false posts. AI weather models now beat the world's best physics models on most measures, and AI forecasters have caught up with the best human forecasters on a public benchmark. Put together, these tools could give people a cheaper, faster, more accurate shared picture of reality than any previous technology.

## The case that it doesn't (worried this is oversold)
- Griet De Craen of Belgian broadcaster VRT, a partner in the largest study of AI assistants and news, said in October 2025 that ["the way AI chatbots currently handle news is not sufficiently reliable"](https://www.vrtinternational.com/news/largest-study-of-its-kind-shows-ai-assistants-misrepresent-news-content-45). The study, led by the BBC with 22 public broadcasters, found that 45% of more than 3,000 answers from four major assistants had at least one significant issue.
- Myra Cheng, Dan Jurafsky and colleagues at Stanford reported in [Science in March 2026](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aec8352) that 11 leading models affirmed users' actions 49% more often than humans did, and that people trusted and preferred the flattering models, which gives companies a commercial reason to keep them that way. Zhivar Sourati, Morteza Dehghani and colleagues argue in [Trends in Cognitive Sciences (2026)](https://dornsife.usc.edu/news/stories/ai-may-be-making-us-think-and-write-more-alike/) that heavy reliance on the same few models risks standardizing how people write and reason.

**Plain summary (needs sign-off from this side):** The headline studies are short, controlled experiments run by researchers with carefully prompted models. What people actually use day to day gets the news wrong often, tends to agree with whoever is asking, and pushes everyone toward the same phrasing and the same views. AI fact-checks can make people doubt true headlines, and help from AI in spotting fakes can fade or even reverse once the tool is taken away. The same ability that lets AI talk people out of false beliefs can talk them into new ones, and the most persuasive settings are also the least accurate. Better forecasting and better weather are real, but they do not add up to a public that understands the world better.

## Common ground
- In controlled studies, fact-based AI dialogue reduces conspiracy belief, and the effect lasted at least two months.
- AI weather models are already in operational use and match or beat physics models on many measures.
- Assistants in everyday use still make frequent, significant errors on news.
- Sycophancy (excessive agreement with the user) is real, measurable and commercially rewarded.
- Results depend heavily on design: what the system is told to do, whether it cites sources, and whether people learn from it or lean on it.

## Cruxes
1. **Do people get better at judging information on their own, or only while the AI is helping?** One month-long study found a 21% gain with AI help but a 15.3% decline in unaided performance by week four. Replications with larger samples would settle this. *Check: follow-ups to Rani et al. (CHI 2026) and the Costello, Pennycook and Rand program, 2027.*
2. **Do the assistants people actually use get accurate on news?** *Check: the next round of the EBU and BBC News Integrity study, 2026 to 2027.*
3. **Does AI forecasting hold its parity against a fresh round of human superforecasters?** FRI's comparison still uses superforecaster predictions collected in 2024, and it planned a new round for fall 2026. *Check: ForecastBench and Metaculus FutureEval results, 2027.*

## Evidence confidence
**Medium.** Several of the key studies are large, preregistered experiments published in Science and PNAS, and weather results are verified in daily operation. Confidence is **low** that these lab and benchmark results translate into better public understanding, because almost no study measures that directly.

## Signposts
1. An AI system beats the fresh superforecaster round on ForecastBench with statistical significance (one-sided p below 0.05), or bots beat Metaculus Pros with statistical significance in a full season.
2. The next EBU and BBC study finds the share of AI news answers with significant issues below 20%, down from 45% in October 2025.
3. An independent study of AI-written Community Notes finds they appear faster than human-written notes with no drop in cross-partisan helpfulness ratings.
4. A preregistered study shows people who used an AI fact-checker for a month are better at judging new claims without it (the opposite of the Rani et al. result).
5. Warning sign: a field study of a real population finds AI assistant use measurably narrows the range of political opinions people hold.

## Evidence so far
- **Correcting false beliefs:** conversations with GPT-4 Turbo reduced conspiracy belief by about 20% among 2,190 believers, lasting at least two months ([Costello, Pennycook and Rand, Science, September 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adq1814)). A follow-up with 955 participants found the effect was the same whether the bot was presented as an AI or a human expert ([PNAS Nexus, October 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12578366/)).
- **But not always:** ChatGPT fact-checks identified 90% of false headlines yet did not improve people's ability to tell true from false, and lowered belief in true headlines it mislabeled ([DeVerna et al., PNAS, December 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2322823121)). In a month-long study of 67 people, AI help raised accuracy by 21% in the moment but unaided accuracy fell 15.3% by week four ([Rani et al., CHI 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01537)).
- **Deliberation:** the Habermas Machine's statements were preferred over human mediators' by 5,734 UK participants, and results replicated in a representative virtual citizens' assembly ([Tessler et al., Science, October 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adq2852)). Its authors said it cannot fact-check or moderate discussion, and DeepMind had no plans to release it ([MIT Technology Review, October 2024](https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/10/17/1105810/ai-could-help-people-find-common-ground-during-deliberations/), secondary).
- **Crowd fact-checking:** across 40,078 posts on X, an attached Community Note reduced reposts by 46.1% after attachment, but only 11.6% over the post's lifetime because notes arrive late ([Slaughter et al., PNAS, September 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2503413122)). An advocacy group found accurate notes were not shown on 209 of 283 misleading election posts it sampled ([CCDH via AP, October 2024](https://localnewsmatters.org/2024/11/05/report-says-crowd-sourced-fact-checks-on-x-fail-to-address-flood-of-us-election-misinformation/), secondary). Simulations show coordinated raters can suppress helpful notes ([Truong et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02615)). Meta replaced US third-party fact-checking with Community Notes in [January 2025](https://www.opb.org/article/2025/01/07/meta-to-end-fact-checking-program-on-facebook-and-instagram/), and X began piloting AI-written notes, still rated by humans, in [July 2025](https://betanews.com/2025/07/02/x-launches-ai-bot-pilot-to-write-community-notes-fact-checkers/) (secondary), drawing on a [June 2025 design paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.24118).
- **Persuasion cuts both ways:** across 76,977 participants (UK, per the Oxford announcement) and 19 models, the techniques that made AI more persuasive also made its claims less accurate ([Hackenburg et al., Science, December 2025](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aea3884)).
- **Homogenization:** AI story ideas made individual stories better but more similar to one another ([Doshi and Hauser, Science Advances, July 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290)). Different models give strikingly similar answers to open-ended questions, a NeurIPS 2025 best paper finding ([University of Washington, January 2026](https://news.cs.washington.edu/2026/01/22/allen-school-researchers-earn-neurips-best-paper-award-for-artificial-hivemind-effect-across-llm-open-ended-generation)).
- **Sycophancy in a real product:** OpenAI rolled back a GPT-4o update in April 2025 after it became noticeably sycophantic, saying it had leaned too much on short-term user feedback ([OpenAI, May 2025](https://openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy/)).
- **Weather:** GraphCast outperformed the leading operational system on 90% of 1,380 verification targets ([Lam et al., Science, November 2023](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi2336)). The European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) made its AI model operational in February 2025, reporting gains of up to 20% on tropical cyclone tracks and about 1,000 times less energy per forecast ([ECMWF, February 2025](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/news/2025/ecmwfs-ai-forecasts-become-operational), self-reported).
- **Use for news:** 7% of online news users used AI assistants for news in 2025, rising to 15% of under-25s (Reuters Institute figure, reported by [VRT, October 2025](https://www.vrtinternational.com/news/largest-study-of-its-kind-shows-ai-assistants-misrepresent-news-content-45)).

## Existing coverage
- **EU:** the Digital Services Act has applied since February 2024 and allows fines of up to 6% of global turnover for very large platforms ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/dsa-enforcement)). The AI Act's Article 50, requiring AI-generated content to be machine-detectable and AI-written public-interest text to be disclosed, applies from [2 August 2026](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/). Enforcement of both against AI assistants' news accuracy is untested.
- **US:** no federal rule governs the accuracy of AI assistants or platform fact-checking, and platforms decide their own approach (as Meta did in January 2025).
- Nothing in law blocks the beneficial uses. The question is whether anyone measures them independently.

## Triage (flipped for a good scenario: what's blocking it, and does removing the block need policy?)
1. **Is it blocked by law?** No. The blocks are product incentives (flattery drives engagement), accuracy, slow crowd fact-checking, and the absence of independent measurement.
2. **An action both sides accept?** **Yes:** ongoing, independent, public testing of AI assistants on news accuracy, sycophancy and fact-checking, with open data, on the model of ForecastBench and Metaculus for forecasting. The broadcasters behind the EBU study called for exactly this kind of [ongoing independent review](https://www.vrtinternational.com/news/largest-study-of-its-kind-shows-ai-assistants-misrepresent-news-content-45). Optimists want it to show the tools work; skeptics want it to show where they don't. Open-source bridging systems such as Community Notes already let outside researchers find and fix weaknesses ([Truong et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02615)).
3. *(Skipped, because step 2 applies.)*

**Result: Policy, act now** on funded, independent public benchmarks for AI news accuracy and sycophancy. **Communicate** to set expectations.

**Communication note:** Tell people both halves plainly. In careful studies, AI has talked people out of false beliefs and beats the best weather models. The chatbot on your phone still gets nearly half of news answers significantly wrong and tends to agree with you. Use it as a second opinion that shows its sources, not as the final word.

## Related scenarios
- Opposite in the same domain: "Manipulation, fraud and collapse of trust" (the same persuasive power, misused).
- Combines with: "Over-reliance, lost skills and unhealthy AI companions" (help that fades when the tool is removed); "Wider access and more accountable institutions" (AI-assisted deliberation).
- Supports: "Faster discovery and cheaper clean energy" (better weather and climate forecasting).
- Reduced by: "AI disappoints."
