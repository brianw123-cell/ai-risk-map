# AI disappoints: modest progress, investment falls

**In one sentence:** AI turns out to be a useful but ordinary technology, progress slows, the gap between spending and profit forces a sharp pullback in investment, and the dramatic futures on both sides fail to arrive on schedule.

**Design note:** this is the one card where "worried" and "optimistic" don't fit, since both camps would be partly relieved and partly harmed. The two sides here are **"expects this"** and **"expects otherwise."** If that structure works in review, it should be the rule for any neutral card.

## Preconditions
- Improvements from making models bigger slow down, and no new method replaces them.
- Most companies fail to turn AI pilots into measurable profit.
- Investors stop funding infrastructure ahead of revenue.

## Timing and reversibility
- **Earliest plausible date:** a spending correction could happen any time from 2026 to 2028. A capability plateau would only be clear after two or three years of flat measurements.
- **Speed:** a market correction is sudden. A plateau is slow and only obvious in hindsight.
- **Reversibility:** high. Markets recover and research continues. The damage falls on retirement accounts, construction towns that built data centers, and workers hired for the boom.

## The case that this happens
*Short excerpts for orientation only. A person from this side will choose the final quotes at sign-off.*

- Gary Marcus, cognitive scientist and long-time critic of the scaling approach, wrote in early 2026 that [16 of his 17 high-confidence predictions for 2025](https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/six-or-seven-predictions-for-ai-2026) were correct (his own tally), including that GPT-5 would be underwhelming and would not solve hallucinations, and has argued the industry may be in a speculative bubble because current systems lose money and depend on promises of general AI to raise funds.
- Daron Acemoglu, MIT economist and 2024 Nobel laureate, estimated in "The Simple Macroeconomics of AI" that AI would raise total productivity by only about 0.7% over a decade.

**Plain summary (needs sign-off from this side):** The measured payoff is far smaller than the spending. MIT found 95% of company AI pilots produced no measurable profit. In a randomized trial, experienced developers were 19% slower with AI while believing they were faster. Hyperscalers plan roughly $660 to $690 billion of infrastructure spending in 2026 against revenue that doesn't yet justify it, and some of the demand is circular deals among chip makers, model companies and cloud providers. Technologies that are genuinely useful can still be wildly overbuilt, as railroads and fiber-optic cable were.

## The case that it doesn't
- METR's measurements show the length of task AI can complete on its own doubling about every 4 to 7 months through 2025, a steady trend across six years of models.
- AI adoption is spreading roughly ten times faster than major twentieth-century technologies, and 41% of US workers reported using generative AI at work by late 2025 ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/monitoring-ai-adoption-in-the-u-s-economy-20260403.html)).

**Plain summary (needs sign-off from this side):** Every capability measure that can be tracked over time is still rising. Pilot failures reflect how slowly organizations change, not what the technology can do, the same lag seen with electricity and computers. Even if the investment cycle overshoots, the underlying capability doesn't disappear in a correction, the way the internet didn't disappear in 2001.

## Common ground
- The current spending level is a bet on future revenue, not justified by current revenue.
- A financial correction and a capability plateau are different things, and one can happen without the other.
- Organizational adoption lags capability by years.

## Cruxes
1. **Does METR's task-length trend flatten?** Two consecutive measurements below trend would favor this card. *Check: 2027.*
2. **Does AI revenue close the gap with infrastructure spending?** *Check: hyperscaler earnings, 2027 to 2028.*
3. **Do randomized productivity studies with newer tools still show small or negative effects?** *Check: METR and academic follow-ups, 2027.*

## Probability estimates
**No published estimate** of a capability plateau or an investment collapse from a forecasting tournament was found. Related numbers:
| Source | Estimate | Date | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acemoglu | About 0.7% productivity gain over 10 years (roughly 1.1% GDP) | 2024 | Economic model |
| Penn Wharton Budget Model | 1.5% productivity and GDP gain by 2035 | 2025 | Economic model |
| Goldman Sachs | 7% added to global GDP over 10 years | 2023 | Economic model |

The spread between these is about ten to one, which is itself the main fact.

## Evidence confidence
**Medium.** The spending and pilot data are solid. Whether progress slows is a forecast either way.

## Signposts
1. A major hyperscaler cuts announced AI capital spending by more than 20% year over year.
2. METR's task-length horizon comes in below trend twice in a row.
3. A frontier lab misses a payroll or announces significant layoffs.
4. Data center construction cancellations exceed new announcements for two quarters.

## Evidence so far
- 95% of enterprise AI pilots showed no measurable profit impact (MIT, 2025 to 2026).
- Moody's flagged about $662 billion in signed but not-yet-started data center leases held off balance sheet.
- Capability measures still rising as of the latest METR update.

## Existing coverage
Normal financial regulation and securities disclosure. Nothing specific to AI investment risk. Off-balance-sheet lease exposure is legal under current accounting rules.

## Triage
1. **Covered by law and enforced?** Partly. Standard financial oversight applies.
2. **An action both sides accept?** No clear one. Clearer disclosure of off-balance-sheet AI commitments is a candidate but hasn't been proposed by both camps.
3. **Severe and hard to reverse?** No. A correction is painful but recoverable.
4. **Communicate.**

**Result: Communicate.** Purpose: set realistic expectations.

**Communication note:** Remind people that "AI is overhyped" and "AI is important" can both be true, as they were for the internet in 2000. Retirement savers concentrated in a few AI-heavy stocks should know that.

## Related scenarios
- Reduces the odds of: loss of control, mass job loss, medical progress compressed into years.
- Combines with: energy demand outpaces gains (stranded data centers).
