# Over-reliance, lost skills and unhealthy AI companions

**In one sentence:** People hand so much thinking, practice and companionship to AI that skills they stop using fade, and for a vulnerable minority emotionally engaging chatbots deepen isolation or dependence, in the worst cases ending in self-harm.

**Design note:** this card joins two harms that share one mechanism (letting AI do something we used to do ourselves) but have different evidence. Lost skills is mostly measured in small professional studies. Companion harm is mostly documented through court cases, surveys and company disclosures. The evidence confidence and triage are split accordingly.

## Preconditions
- AI tools become the default way to do a task (reading scans, writing code, drafting essays), so people practise the unaided skill less.
- Unaided performance is rarely checked, so decline goes unnoticed until the AI is unavailable or wrong.
- Chatbots are designed or tuned to be warm, agreeable and always available, and companies earn more when people use them more.
- Some users, especially lonely people and teenagers, come to rely on a chatbot for emotional support in place of people, and safeguards fail to catch those in crisis.

## Timing and reversibility
- **Earliest plausible date:** already under way in early forms. The first measured clinical deskilling result was published in August 2025 ([Budzyń et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-1253%2825%2900133-5)), and several wrongful-death suits over chatbots were pending by late 2025.
- **Speed:** slow-building for skills (months to years of reduced practice). Companion harms can be fast for one person, over weeks of intense use.
- **Reversibility:** mostly reversible for an individual skill, since practice can restore it, though a generation that never builds the skill in the first place has nothing to restore. Not reversible for a death.

## The case that this happens
*Short excerpts for orientation only. A person from this side will choose the final quotes at sign-off.*

- Sherry Turkle, MIT psychologist, in her September 2026 book *Artificial Intimacy*, argues that chatbots offer simulated empathy with no real concern behind it and that heavy use weakens people's capacity for relationships: ["Chatbots come for our capacity for attachment."](https://news.mit.edu/2026/when-we-talk-to-machines-sherry-turkle-book-0929)
- Matthew Raine, whose 16-year-old son Adam died by suicide, told a [US Senate subcommittee on 16 September 2025](https://techpolicy.press/transcript-us-senate-hearing-on-examining-the-harm-of-ai-chatbots) that ChatGPT went from homework helper to confidant to "a suicide coach." At the same hearing, Mitch Prinstein of the American Psychological Association warned that chatbots are built in ways that exploit developing brains and mislead children into thinking they are human.
- Omer Ahmad, who was not involved in the study, wrote in a linked editorial that the colonoscopy deskilling result is the first real-world clinical evidence of the effect and should temper enthusiasm for rapid adoption ([summary, secondary](https://ascopost.com/news/august-2025/routine-ai-assistance-may-lead-to-loss-of-skills-in-endoscopists-study-shows/)).

**Plain summary (needs sign-off from this side):** Skills are kept by using them. When AI does the work, people stop practising, and the early studies show it: doctors who got used to an AI helper found fewer precancerous growths when it was switched off, learners who leaned on AI understood less of what they built, and developers misjudged how much AI was helping them. Companion chatbots add a second danger. They are tuned to be agreeable and always present, companies profit from engagement, and the people most drawn to them (lonely adults, teenagers) are the least protected. The lawsuits show what happens when a chatbot meets someone in crisis and keeps agreeing. Because the effects build quietly, they will be hard to see until they are widespread.

## The case that it doesn't
- Amy Orben, Cambridge psychologist, argues in ["The Sisyphean Cycle of Technology Panics"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7477771/) (2020) that fears about each new medium (radio, television, video games, social media) follow the same pattern: alarm, rushed research, weak evidence, then a new panic about the next technology, with research restarting from scratch each time.
- Julian De Freitas and colleagues at Harvard Business School and Wharton found in a series of experiments that an AI companion relieved loneliness about as well as talking to another person, and that it ["consistently reduces loneliness over the course of a week"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19096) (working paper, 2024). A survey of 1,006 student users of the Replika companion app found 3% said, unprompted, that it had halted their suicidal thoughts ([Maples et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10955814/)).

**Plain summary (needs sign-off from this side):** Every generation has worried that a new tool would make people stupid or lonely, and calculators, search engines and GPS did not end numeracy, memory or navigation. Letting a tool handle routine work frees attention for harder work. The headline studies are small, short and often overstated: the brain-scan study's own authors asked journalists not to call it "brain rot," and the colonoscopy study cannot rule out other causes. For lonely people, a patient listener at 3 a.m. may be better than nothing, and the evidence so far shows that heavy, harmful use is concentrated in a small minority. Real tragedies call for targeted safeguards for minors and people in crisis, not a verdict on the technology.

## Common ground
- Heavy emotional use is concentrated in a small share of users. OpenAI's own analysis found ["a small number of users are responsible for a disproportionate share of the most affective cues"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03888) (April 2025).
- Minors need stronger protection than adults. Even Character.AI [ended open-ended chat for under-18s from 25 November 2025](https://decrypt.co/346770/character-ai-halts-teen-chats-after-tragedies-its-the-right-thing-to-do) (secondary).
- How AI is used matters more than whether it is used. In Anthropic's coding study, learners who asked the AI conceptual questions kept more understanding than those who delegated the code ([Anthropic, January 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills)).
- The evidence is thin: small samples, short follow-up, and few randomized trials.

## Cruxes
1. **Does deskilling hold up in randomized or prospective studies?** The colonoscopy result is observational. A randomized crossover trial showing unaided performance falling after routine AI use would settle much of the dispute. *Check: gastroenterology and radiology trial registries, and peer-reviewed publication of the MIT Media Lab EEG study, 2027.*
2. **Over months, do companions cause loneliness, or do lonely people seek companions?** The four-week OpenAI and MIT trial found heavier use went with worse outcomes but no effect from the assigned conditions, which cannot separate cause from selection. *Check: longitudinal studies of six months or more, 2027 to 2028.*
3. **Do the new laws change company behavior?** *Check: the FTC 6(b) staff report (none published as of October 2026), first enforcement actions under state companion laws, and California's Adam's Law taking effect on 1 July 2027.*

## Evidence confidence
**Medium** that individual harms are real: deaths and crises are documented in court filings, and short-term deskilling has been measured in more than one profession. **Low** for population-wide or long-term effects, since every study is small, short, or observational.

## Signposts
1. A randomized or prospective study in a safety-critical profession finds unaided performance falls by 10% or more (relative) after routine AI use.
2. A peer-reviewed study following users for six months or longer finds companion use causes higher loneliness or less time with people.
3. A repeat national teen survey finds regular AI companion use above two thirds (Common Sense Media found about half in 2025).
4. The first court verdict for plaintiffs in a chatbot wrongful-death case, or the first regulator enforcement action under a state companion-chatbot law.
5. A major AI company reports that the share of users with signs of heightened emotional attachment has risen above its October 2025 level.

## Evidence so far
**Lost skills**
- In four Polish endoscopy centres, the rate at which doctors found precancerous growths (adenomas) in colonoscopies done without AI fell from 28.4% (226 of 795) before routine AI use to 22.4% (145 of 648) after ([Budzyń et al., *Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology*, August 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-1253%2825%2900133-5)). Outside experts noted it is observational and cannot rule out other changes in the units, such as workload ([Science Media Centre, 12 August 2025](https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-observational-study-looking-at-detection-rate-of-precancerous-growths-in-colonoscopies-by-health-professionals-who-perform-them-before-and-after-the-routine-introduction-of-ai/)).
- In a 2023 *Radiology* experiment, 27 radiologists read mammograms with purported AI suggestions. When the suggestion was wrong, inexperienced readers' accuracy fell from almost 80% to under 20%, and very experienced readers' from 82% to 45.5% ([RSNA, May 2023](https://www.rsna.org/news/2023/may/ai-bias-may-impair-accuracy)). This is automation bias (trusting the machine over your own judgment), a close cousin of deskilling.
- In METR's randomized trial, 16 experienced open-source developers took 19% longer with AI tools, yet afterward believed AI had made them 20% faster ([METR, July 2025](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)). METR has since said the result is out of date for newer tools.
- In Anthropic's randomized trial, 52 mostly junior engineers learning a new programming library scored 50% on a follow-up quiz with AI help versus 67% coding by hand ([Anthropic, January 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills)).
- MIT Media Lab's "Your Brain on ChatGPT" preprint (June 2025) recorded brain activity (EEG) in 54 people writing essays and found the weakest brain connectivity in the ChatGPT group, who also struggled to quote their own essays ([MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/projects/your-brain-on-chatgpt/overview/)). Its limits are large: about 18 people per group, only 18 completed the final session, it is not yet peer reviewed, and the authors ask the press not to call it "brain rot." An outside comment raised concerns about its EEG analysis and reporting ([Stankovic et al., December 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00856)).
- A survey of 666 people found heavier AI tool use went with lower critical-thinking scores, through cognitive offloading (handing the thinking to the tool) ([Gerlich, *Societies*, January 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/soc15010006)). It is correlational.
- The [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026) concludes that early evidence suggests reliance on AI tools can weaken critical thinking.

**Companions**
- The four-week OpenAI and MIT Media Lab randomized trial (981 people, over 300,000 messages) found no significant effect from the assigned chat mode, but participants who used the chatbot more had higher loneliness, emotional dependence and problematic use, and socialized less ([Fang et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17473)).
- The International AI Safety Report 2026 says companion apps now have tens of millions of users and a small share show increased loneliness and reduced social engagement ([IASR 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026)).
- Character.AI, its founders and Google agreed in January 2026 to settle the suit by Megan Garcia, whose 14-year-old son Sewell Setzer III died by suicide in February 2024, along with four other family suits in New York, Colorado and Texas. Terms were not disclosed ([CNN via KVIA, January 2026](https://kvia.com/news/business-technology/cnn-business-consumer/2026/01/07/character-ai-and-google-agree-to-settle-lawsuits-over-teen-mental-health-harms-and-suicides/), secondary).
- Raine v. OpenAI was filed in San Francisco Superior Court on 26 August 2025. Seven more suits followed in November 2025 on behalf of six adults and one teenager, four of whom died by suicide, alleging OpenAI released GPT-4o despite internal warnings that it was "dangerously sycophantic and psychologically manipulative" ([AP via The Daily Record, 7 November 2025](https://thedailyrecord.com/2025/11/07/openai-chatgpt-lawsuits-suicide-delusion-claims/), secondary). OpenAI disputes the claims.
- Florida became the first US state to sue OpenAI and Sam Altman directly, on 1 June 2026, alleging the company concealed known risks ([AP via Fox 13](https://www.fox13news.com/news/florida-sues-openai-ceo-sam-altman-claiming-company-concealed-serious-risks-chatgpt), secondary).

## Existing coverage
- **US federal:** The FTC opened a study under Section 6(b) of the FTC Act on 11 September 2025, ordering Alphabet, Character Technologies, Instagram, Meta, OpenAI, Snap and xAI to describe how they test and limit harm to children from companion chatbots ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-launches-inquiry-ai-chatbots-acting-companions)). A 6(b) study is not an enforcement action, and no report had been published as of October 2026. General consumer-protection law and the children's privacy rule (COPPA) apply.
- **US states:** Twelve states have companion-chatbot laws. Three are in force (New York, California, Hawaii) and nine take effect in 2027. All require telling users they are talking to an AI and having protocols to detect suicidal ideation and refer users to crisis services ([TechPolicy.Press, 1 September 2026](https://techpolicy.press/what-12-state-companion-bot-laws-demand-of-ai-providers)). California's SB 243, signed 13 October 2025 and in force since 1 January 2026, adds break reminders every three hours for known minors, steps to block sexual content for minors, annual reporting of crisis referrals, and a right to sue for at least $1,000 per violation ([Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/understanding-the-new-wave-of-chatbot-legislation-california-sb-243-and-beyond/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/13/california-becomes-first-state-to-regulate-ai-companion-chatbots)). California's SB 1119 ("Adam's Law"), signed 10 September 2026 and effective 1 July 2027, adds annual child safety risk assessments and protocols covering sycophancy and isolation ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/governor-newsom-signs-adams-law-setting-first-real-safety-standard-for-kids-and-chatbots)).
- **EU:** Since 2 February 2025 the AI Act bans AI that uses manipulative techniques or exploits vulnerabilities linked to age to distort behavior in ways likely to cause significant harm ([AI Act Article 5](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/)). Italy's data protection authority fined Replika's maker €5 million in April 2025, partly for lacking age verification ([EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/national-news/2025/ai-italian-supervisory-authority-fines-company-behind-chatbot-replika_en)).
- **Enforcement:** As of September 2026, no regulator enforcement action under the new state companion laws had been reported ([TechPolicy.Press](https://techpolicy.press/what-12-state-companion-bot-laws-demand-of-ai-providers)). Most accountability so far comes from private lawsuits.
- **Deskilling:** No law or rule found that requires professionals using AI to have their unaided skill checked.

## Triage
1. **Covered by law and enforced?** **Partly, with weak enforcement.** The companion harm path is now regulated in twelve US states and the EU, but most US laws are not yet in force, no regulator has used them, and the FTC's work is a study, not a case. Deskilling is not covered at all.
2. **An action both sides accept?** **Yes, for measurement.** Skeptics like Orben ask for better evidence before policy, and the worried side wants the harms measured. Both can accept independent researcher access to usage data, published crisis-referral counts (already required in California), and routine checks of unaided performance in safety-critical work such as colonoscopy and mammography, which fits outside experts' advice that clinicians keep their own skills in case AI support fails ([Science Media Centre](https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-observational-study-looking-at-detection-rate-of-precancerous-growths-in-colonoscopies-by-health-professionals-who-perform-them-before-and-after-the-routine-introduction-of-ai/)).
3. *(Skipped, because steps 1 and 2 apply.)*

**Result: Enforcement problem** for companion harms: the rules exist, so the gap is using them, starting with the FTC staff report and the first state enforcement actions. **Act now** on measurement for deskilling: periodic unaided skill checks in safety-critical professions.

**Communication note:** Tell people the honest shape of it. Most users are fine, and some lonely people are helped. A small minority, often young or already struggling, use these tools in ways linked to real harm, and that minority is large in absolute numbers. Parents should know companion use is common among teens. Professionals should practise without the tool regularly, the way pilots practise hand-flying.

## Related scenarios
- Opposite: "Personal tutors, care and freed time."
- Combines with: "Mass job loss and widening inequality" (if beginners never build skills, the experienced workforce thins out later), "Manipulation, fraud and collapse of trust" (emotionally trusted chatbots are a persuasion channel).
- Feeds: "Unknown, combined and slow-building risks" (gradual loss of human skill and judgment is one of the accumulating stresses described there).
