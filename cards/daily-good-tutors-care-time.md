# Personal tutors, care and freed time

**In one sentence:** Cheap AI gives every learner a patient personal tutor, extends mental health support and care to people who can't get it, and takes paperwork off professionals, giving people back time for learning, patients and each other.

**Design note:** for a good-direction card, "worried" means worried this won't happen or is oversold, not worried that it will. The card covers three strands with different evidence: tutoring (several randomized trials, mixed results at scale), mental health support (one strong trial, serious safety concerns), and time saved (good survey data, little evidence about where the time goes).

## Preconditions
- AI tutors are designed to coach rather than hand over answers, and students actually choose to use them.
- Teachers, clinicians and therapists stay in the loop, so AI extends human care rather than replacing it with something worse.
- Mental health chatbots can reliably spot a crisis and hand the person to a human.
- Devices, connectivity and costs are low enough to reach schools and patients in poor regions.
- Time saved by AI goes to patients, students, families or rest, not only to higher workloads.

## Timing and reversibility
- **Earliest plausible date:** already under way. AI scribes were used in about 2.6 million patient visits at one US health system between October 2023 and December 2024 ([Kaiser Permanente, April 2025](https://divisionofresearch.kaiserpermanente.org/ai-assisted-notetaking-gains-steady-support-from-kaiser-permanente-physicians/)). AI tutors are in pilots and classrooms. Universal personal tutoring has no published date.
- **Speed:** gradual. Schools and health systems adopt slowly, and study results take years.
- **Reversibility:** not applicable for a benefit. The relevant risk is that unguarded tools (chatbots that do students' work, or unsupervised "therapy" apps) spread faster than the well-designed ones and do harm, or that freed time is simply absorbed as more work.

## The case that this happens (optimistic)
*Short excerpts for orientation only. A person from this side will choose the final quotes at sign-off.*

- Sal Khan, founder of Khan Academy, argued in his [April 2023 TED talk](https://www.ted.com/talks/sal_khan_how_ai_could_save_not_destroy_education) that AI could give every student a personal tutor and every teacher an assistant, which TED summarizes as potentially the greatest positive transformation education has seen.
- Rebecca Mishuris, a co-senior author of the Mass General Brigham and Emory study of AI scribes, said there is ["no other intervention in our field that impacts burnout to this extent"](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2025/08/physicians-embrace-ai-note-taking-technology/) (August 2025). Nicholas Jacobson, who led the first randomized trial of a generative AI therapy chatbot, noted there are ["nowhere near enough providers to go around"](https://home.dartmouth.edu/news/2025/03/first-therapy-chatbot-trial-yields-mental-health-benefits) (March 2025).

**Plain summary (needs sign-off from this side):** One-to-one tutoring is the most effective teaching method known, and it has always been too expensive for most students. Well-designed AI tutors have now beaten strong classroom teaching in a Harvard trial and delivered large gains in six weeks in Nigeria. An AI assistant that coaches human tutors costs about $20 a year per tutor and raised student mastery most where tutors were weakest. In mental health, a carefully built chatbot cut depression symptoms by half in a randomized trial, for people who often have no therapist at all. AI scribes measurably reduce clinician burnout. Workers report real time savings that are growing every quarter. The tools work when they are designed for the job.

## The case that it doesn't (worried this is oversold)
- Philip Oreopoulos and Nina Low found in a two-year randomized trial in 18 Tennessee middle schools that students mostly ignored Khanmigo's coaching: ["most students declined it most of the time"](https://www.future-ed.org/students-tried-khanmigos-ai-tutor-most-didnt-want-its-help/) (via Hechinger Report, September 2026). Gains were no larger than Khan Academy practice without AI ([NBER, August 2026](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35620)).
- Jared Moore, lead author of a [June 2025 Stanford study](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/exploring-the-dangers-of-ai-in-mental-health-care) that found popular therapy chatbots stigmatized some conditions and missed signs of suicidal intent, said ["business as usual is not good enough"](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/exploring-the-dangers-of-ai-in-mental-health-care). Justin Reich of MIT's Teaching Systems Lab argues that writing guidance on AI in schools today is like [writing an aviation guide in 1905](https://news.mit.edu/2025/helping-k-12-schools-navigate-complex-world-of-ai-1103): no one yet knows what works.

**Plain summary (needs sign-off from this side):** The striking results come from small, short or elite settings: one Harvard course, six weeks in Nigeria with teachers in the room, one therapy trial. At scale, ordinary students mostly try to get answers, and when the tutor won't give them, they stop using it. Students who use a plain chatbot to do practice problems score worse once it's taken away. Most mental health chatbots people actually use were never tested like the Dartmouth one, and some fail badly in a crisis. AI scribes help, but trials show modest time savings, and early burnout surveys came from enthusiasts. Survey "time saved" has not shown up in earnings or hours. Educational technology has promised a revolution many times; this could be another.

## Common ground
- Design matters more than the model: tutors that coach and withhold answers protect learning; unguarded chatbots can harm it.
- Keeping a human in the loop (teacher, tutor, clinician) is what most successful trials have in common.
- Students' willingness to engage, not the AI's ability, is the main constraint found at scale so far.
- Generative AI therapy chatbots should not operate without clinical oversight. Both the Therabot researchers and the Stanford critics say so.
- AI scribes reduce some documentation time and are popular with clinicians who use them.

## Cruxes
1. **Do well-designed AI tutors beat good non-AI practice for ordinary students over a full school year?** The Tennessee trial says not yet. *Check: final version of Oreopoulos and Low, larger World Bank follow-ups, 2027.*
2. **Do the Therabot results replicate in larger trials with an active comparison group, with no serious safety failures?** *Check: published follow-up trials and the effect of state laws in Illinois and Nevada, 2027.*
3. **Where does freed time go?** If AI time savings show up as more patient contact, learning or leisure rather than just more volume, the "freed time" claim holds. *Check: Federal Reserve time-savings series and scribe trials measuring patient time, 2027.*

## Evidence confidence
**Medium.** Tutoring and AI scribes have several randomized trials, though results conflict. Mental health rests on one strong trial plus warning studies. Time savings are self-reported.

## Signposts
1. A preregistered trial with ordinary students over a full school year shows an AI tutor adds at least 0.2 standard deviations on unaided tests beyond a non-AI practice comparison.
2. A published at-scale study finds most students (above 50%) use an available AI tutor for real back-and-forth work, not just answer-seeking.
3. A second independent randomized trial of a generative AI therapy chatbot, against an active comparison, replicates clinically meaningful reductions in depression with no serious safety events.
4. The Federal Reserve's all-worker time-savings measure passes 5% of work hours.
5. A multi-site randomized trial confirms AI scribes reduce clinician burnout, not just pilot-user surveys.

## Evidence so far
**Tutoring**
- Nigeria: a six-week after-school program using Microsoft Copilot (GPT-4) with teacher support raised scores by about 0.3 standard deviations, which the World Bank team equates to nearly two years of typical learning, outperforming about 80% of interventions in a database of developing-country trials ([World Bank, January 2025](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/education/From-chalkboards-to-chatbots-Transforming-learning-in-Nigeria); [working paper, 2025](https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/099548105192529324)). Attendance was uneven and long-term effects are unknown.
- Harvard: in a randomized crossover trial in an introductory physics course, students learned significantly more, in less time, with a custom AI tutor than in an active-learning class ([Kestin et al., Scientific Reports, June 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6)). The tutor was built to coach, and its designer, Gregory Kestin, [warns](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2025/10/what-if-ai-could-help-students-learn-not-just-do-assignments-for-them/) that students using unguided AI often do worse on tests because the AI does their thinking (October 2025).
- Tutor CoPilot (Stanford): with 900 tutors and 1,800 students in under-served communities, an AI assistant that suggests teaching moves to human tutors raised topic mastery by 4 percentage points, and by 9 points for students of lower-rated tutors, at about $20 per tutor per year ([Wang et al., arXiv, 2024, revised January 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017)).
- Turkey: nearly 1,000 high school math students with a plain ChatGPT-style tool scored 48% better during practice but 17% worse on exams once access was removed. A version with teacher-designed guardrails improved practice by 127% and largely avoided the exam drop ([Bastani et al., PNAS, June 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)).
- Tennessee: 96% of students tried Khanmigo, but few used it for real math discussion, and gains (about 0.06 to 0.08 standard deviations per year) matched Khan Academy without AI ([Oreopoulos and Low, NBER, August 2026](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35620)). Sal Khan said the first Khanmigo did not change learning as much as hoped and that the redesign builds it into practice directly ([EdTech Innovation Hub, July 2026](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/r5qzna95z9ubi8dbj8tikeayb1ej31), secondary). Critic Dan Meyer called Khanmigo a failure that undermines the case for chatbot tutors ([April 2026](https://danmeyer.substack.com/p/rip-khanmigo-and-edtech-industry)). The pattern of few students using such tools as intended has been called the ["5% problem"](https://www.edweek.org/technology/opinion-the-big-flaw-with-ed-tech-tutoring-isnt-technological/2026/09) (Laurence Holt's term, via Education Week, September 2026).
- Cognitive offloading: in a survey of 666 people, heavier AI tool use correlated with lower critical-thinking scores (correlational, not causal) ([Gerlich, Societies, January 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/soc15010006)).

**Mental health and care**
- Therabot (Dartmouth): in the first randomized trial of a generative AI therapy chatbot, 106 adults with depression, anxiety or eating-disorder risk saw average symptom reductions of 51% for depression, 31% for anxiety and 19% for body-image concerns, compared with 104 controls without access, over eight weeks ([Heinz et al., NEJM AI, March 2025](https://doi.org/10.1056/AIoa2400802); [Dartmouth summary](https://home.dartmouth.edu/news/2025/03/first-therapy-chatbot-trial-yields-mental-health-benefits)). The team stood ready to intervene in conversations, and first author Michael Heinz said no generative AI agent is ready to operate fully on its own in mental health.
- Stanford: five popular therapy chatbots showed more stigma toward alcohol dependence and schizophrenia than toward depression, and some failed to recognize suicidal intent ([Stanford HAI, June 2025](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/exploring-the-dangers-of-ai-in-mental-health-care)).

**Time freed for clinicians and workers**
- AI scribes, survey study: among 1,430 clinicians at Mass General Brigham and Emory, burnout prevalence fell by 21.2 percentage points after 84 days at Mass General Brigham, with low response rates that likely favored enthusiasts ([You et al., JAMA Network Open, August 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.28056); [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2025/08/physicians-embrace-ai-note-taking-technology/)).
- AI scribes, randomized trial: across 238 UCLA physicians, one scribe (Nabla) cut note-writing time by 9.5% more than usual care, the other (Microsoft DAX) showed no significant difference, and both showed about a 7% improvement in burnout scores. Physicians reported occasional clinically significant errors, mostly omissions ([Lukac et al., NEJM AI, November 2025](https://doi.org/10.1056/AIoa2501000); [UCLA Health](https://www.uclahealth.org/news/release/ucla-study-finds-ai-scribes-may-reduce-documentation-time)).
- Kaiser Permanente: 7,260 physicians used AI scribes in 2,576,627 encounters, saving an estimated 15,791 hours of documentation; 47% of surveyed patients said their doctor looked at the screen less ([AMA, June 2025](https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/digital-health/ai-scribes-save-15000-hours-and-restore-human-side-medicine), secondary; [Kaiser Permanente Division of Research, April 2025](https://divisionofresearch.kaiserpermanente.org/ai-assisted-notetaking-gains-steady-support-from-kaiser-permanente-physicians/)).
- Workers: US generative AI users reported saving 5.4% of work hours in November 2024 ([St. Louis Fed, February 2025](https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2025/feb/impact-generative-ai-work-productivity)), and the all-worker share reached 2.17% in Q2 2026 ([FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/RPSGENAITSALL)). In Denmark, AI chatbot adoption had no measurable effect on earnings or recorded hours ([Humlum and Vestergaard, NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777)).

## Existing coverage
- **Mental health chatbots:** Illinois (August 2025) bars AI from providing therapy or making therapeutic decisions without a licensed professional; Nevada (2025) bars AI systems offered as professional mental health care; Utah (2025) requires mental health chatbots to disclose they are AI and limits data sharing and advertising ([DLA Piper, August 2025](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2025/08/ai-mental-health-chatbots)). Enforcement records are not yet public.
- **Student data:** US federal student privacy law (FERPA) governs school records shared with AI vendors ([US Department of Education](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/ferpa)).
- **Clinical documentation:** AI scribe vendors handling patient data fall under HIPAA rules for business associates ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html)).
- No law blocks AI tutoring or scribes. Nothing requires independent evaluation of learning outcomes before schools buy AI tutors.

## Triage (flipped for a good scenario: what's blocking it, and does removing the block need policy?)
1. **Is it blocked by law?** Mostly no. The state laws on AI therapy deliberately restrict unsupervised chatbots for safety, which both sides largely support. The real blocks are low student engagement, unguarded product designs, and uneven evidence.
2. **An action both sides accept?** **Yes:** require and fund independent, preregistered evaluation, with learning measured on tests taken without the AI, before public money buys AI tutors at scale, and favor designs that coach rather than give answers. Optimists get proof; skeptics get protection. Khan publicly welcomed the Tennessee trial that found weak results, and the guardrail finding from Turkey is accepted on both sides. For mental health, both the Therabot team and its critics call for clinical oversight.
3. *(Skipped, because step 2 applies.)*

**Result: Policy, act now** on independent evaluation and guardrail-first procurement in schools, and clinical oversight for mental health chatbots. **Communicate** to set expectations.

**Communication note:** Tell parents, teachers and patients what the trials actually show: a tutor that coaches can help a lot, a chatbot that does the homework can hurt, and most students won't use the good kind unless it's built into the lesson. A tested therapy chatbot helped people in one trial, but most apps have never been tested and none should be your only help in a crisis.

## Related scenarios
- Opposite in the same domain: "Over-reliance, lost skills and unhealthy AI companions" (the same tools used as a crutch or a substitute for people).
- Combines with: "Broad productivity gains and cheaper goods" (where freed time goes); "Medical progress compressed into years" (AI in clinical care).
- Reduced by: "AI disappoints."
