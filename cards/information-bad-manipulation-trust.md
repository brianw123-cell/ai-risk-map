# Manipulation, fraud and collapse of trust

**In one sentence:** Cheap fake voices, videos and persuasive chatbots make fraud easier, let campaigns and foreign governments manipulate voters, and leave people unable to trust what they see and hear, including real evidence.

**Design note:** The evidence splits sharply by harm. AI-assisted fraud is measured and growing. AI's effect on elections has so far been small when anyone has measured it. The long-run damage to trust is the least measured and the most disputed. The card keeps the three apart so readers can see where the sides actually disagree.

## Preconditions
- Tools that clone a voice or face, or write tailored persuasive text, become cheap and widely available.
- Criminals and political operators adopt them faster than banks, platforms, courts and ordinary people adapt their checks.
- AI chat assistants become a main way people get information, so whoever shapes them shapes what people believe.
- People learn that anything can be faked, and start dismissing real evidence too.

## Timing and reversibility
- **Earliest plausible date:** already under way for fraud. The FBI logged more than 22,000 complaints referencing AI in 2025. For elections and trust, no measurable damage has been shown yet, and the next test is the November 2026 US midterms.
- **Speed:** gradual, with occasional sharp incidents (a large fraud, a late-campaign fake).
- **Reversibility:** individual fraud losses are rarely recovered. Trust, once lost, rebuilds slowly, but trust in news and institutions was already falling before generative AI, so it is hard to say how much AI adds.

## The case that this happens
*Short excerpts for orientation only. A person from this side will choose the final quotes at sign-off.*

- Law professors Bobby Chesney and Danielle Citron warned in the [*California Law Review*](https://www.californialawreview.org/print/deep-fakes-a-looming-challenge-for-privacy-democracy-and-national-security) (2019) that deepfakes make it "easier for liars to avoid accountability for things that are in fact true." They called this the liar's dividend, and noted it grows as the public learns that fakes exist.
- Francesco Salvi, Robert West and colleagues at EPFL found in a [randomized trial](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14380) (preprint 2024, published in *Nature Human Behaviour* 2025) that people who debated GPT-4, when it was given their basic personal details, had 81.7% higher odds of moving toward the AI's position than people who debated another human.

**Plain summary (needs sign-off from this side):** Fraud is the clearest proof. Criminals already use cloned voices and fake video calls to steal millions, and the FBI's count of AI-related complaints is almost certainly an undercount because victims often don't know AI was involved. Persuasion research shows AI can already out-argue people one on one, especially when it knows something about them, and it can do that for millions of people at once. Elections have not been swung yet, but the tools are improving every year and each election is a new test. The deepest damage is slow: once people believe anything can be faked, real recordings of real wrongdoing lose their force.

## The case that it doesn't
- Felix Simon, Sacha Altay and Hugo Mercier argued in the [*Harvard Kennedy School Misinformation Review*](https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/misinformation-reloaded-fears-about-the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-misinformation-are-overblown/) (October 2023) that "the consumption of misinformation is mostly limited by demand and not by supply," so cheaper fakes add little.
- Sayash Kapoor and Arvind Narayanan of Princeton reviewed all 78 AI election cases in the WIRED AI Elections Project database for the [Knight First Amendment Institute](https://knightcolumbia.org/blog/we-looked-at-78-election-deepfakes-political-misinformation-is-not-an-ai-problem) (December 2024). About half had no deceptive intent, every deceptive one could have been made without AI for a few hundred dollars, and cheap non-AI fakes were far more common.

**Plain summary (needs sign-off from this side):** Fears about each new media technology have repeatedly outrun the evidence. People don't believe things because they saw a convincing fake; they believe what fits what they already think, and plenty of false content was available long before AI. What limits misinformation is getting people's attention, and AI doesn't change that. The 2024 "year of elections" produced far less AI damage than predicted. Fraud is a real problem, but it is a crime problem that existing law covers, and AI is still a small share of reported losses. Overstating the threat has its own cost: it teaches people to distrust everything, which is the liar's dividend by another route.

## Common ground
- AI-assisted fraud is real, growing, and already illegal; the question is enforcement.
- No study has shown a deepfake or AI campaign changing an election result as of October 2026.
- The liar's dividend is real as an idea; the two sides disagree on whether AI makes it much worse.
- Alarmist messaging can itself erode trust, so both sides want accurate public communication.

## Cruxes
1. **Does AI's share of fraud grow quickly?** In 2025, complaints referencing AI accounted for about $893 million of $20.9 billion in losses reported to the FBI. If that share passes 10%, the "small share of a crime problem" reading weakens. *Check: FBI IC3 annual report for 2026, expected spring 2027.*
2. **Do measured election effects stay small as AI chat assistants become a main news source?** The persuasion lab results are strong, the real-world election results are weak. *Check: academic studies of the November 2026 US midterms, through 2027.*
3. **Does "it's a deepfake" start working against real video?** A 2024 study found false misinformation claims helped politicians escape text-based scandals but mostly not video ones. *Check: replications of Schiff, Schiff and Bueno, 2027.*

## Probability estimates
**No published probability** of a collapse of public trust caused by AI was found. The closest numbers measure concern, not likelihood:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [AI Impacts survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02843) | 1,345 AI researchers | "Spread of false information (e.g. deepfakes)" is a substantial or extreme concern | 86% said yes | Oct 2023 | Concern, not probability |

| [WEF Global Risks Report 2026](https://www3.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Global_Risks_Report_2026.pdf) | Over 1,300 experts, surveyed Aug to Sep 2025 | Severity rank of "misinformation and disinformation" among 33 global risks | #2 over two years, #4 over ten years | Jan 2026 | Ranking, not probability |
| Same | Same | Severity rank of "adverse outcomes of AI technologies" | #30 over two years, #5 over ten years | Jan 2026 | Ranking, not probability |

**Read these with care:** these surveys measure how worried experts are, which is not evidence of how likely the harm is. The skeptical researchers on this card argue that exactly this kind of worry has been overstated before.

## Evidence confidence
**Medium.** High for fraud (official complaint data, confirmed corporate cases). Medium for election effects (several independent studies, all finding small effects so far). Low for long-run collapse of trust, which no study has yet measured directly.

## Signposts
1. AI-referenced losses in the FBI's annual IC3 report exceed 10% of total reported losses.
2. A national election result is annulled or formally overturned with a deepfake or AI persuasion campaign named as a main reason.
3. A peer-reviewed study of a real election finds AI-generated content or AI chat persuasion shifted vote shares by more than one percentage point.
4. A court, election authority or major news organization publicly rejects authentic video as fake, and the error is later confirmed.
5. The FTC brings its first enforcement action against a platform under the TAKE IT DOWN Act's 48-hour removal rule.

## Evidence so far
**Fraud**
- Losses reported to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center reached $20.877 billion in 2025, up 26% from 2024, across 1,008,597 complaints. 22,364 complaints referenced AI, with $893 million in losses. Investment fraud with a reported AI link passed $632 million, out of more than $8 billion in total investment fraud, and the FBI notes many victims don't realize AI was involved. Voice-clone "distress" scams accounted for over $5 million ([FBI IC3 2025 Annual Report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf), 2026).
- US consumers reported losing more than $12.5 billion to fraud in 2024, up 25% from 2023 ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/03/new-ftc-data-show-big-jump-reported-losses-fraud-125-billion-2024), March 2025). These FTC figures are not broken out by AI use.
- Engineering firm Arup confirmed that an employee in its Hong Kong office sent HK$200 million (about US$25.6 million) across 15 transactions after a video call in which fraudsters used fake voices and images of senior colleagues ([CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2024/05/16/tech/arup-deepfake-scam-loss-hong-kong-intl-hnk/index.html), May 2024).

**Elections**
- Recorded Future identified 82 deepfakes of public figures in 38 countries between July 2023 and July 2024. Scams were the most common purpose (26.8%), ahead of false statements and electioneering. Examples included a fake audio clip about vote rigging released just before Slovakia's 2023 election ([Recorded Future](https://www.recordedfuture.com/research/targets-objectives-emerging-tactics-political-deepfakes)).
- Meta reported that AI content about elections, politics and social topics was less than 1% of all fact-checked misinformation on its platforms during the major 2024 elections ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/12/2024-global-elections-meta/), December 2024; company figure).
- The Brennan Center concluded that AI did not disrupt the 2024 US election and no measurable effect on the outcome has been identified, while warning that deepfakes were already eroding trust ([Brennan Center](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/gauging-ai-threat-free-and-fair-elections), March 2025).
- A study of 231 deepfakes around the 2024 US presidential election found they spiked, and drew more engagement, before key campaign events ([Postiglione et al., ICWSM](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/42728), 2026).
- In Canada's 2025 election, about 5.86% of election-related images on X, Bluesky and Reddit were deepfakes, but harmful ones drew only 0.12% of views on X ([Livernoche et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13915), December 2025).

**Persuasion**
- Across three experiments with 76,977 participants and 19 AI models, the training and prompting methods that made chatbots more persuasive (by up to 51% and 27%) also made their claims less accurate ([Hackenburg et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13919), July 2025, preprint).
- University of Zurich researchers secretly ran AI accounts on Reddit's r/changemyview debate forum, posting more than a thousand comments over several months, some posing as a rape victim or a trauma counselor and tailoring replies to users' inferred backgrounds. Reddit said it would pursue formal legal demands ([404 Media](https://www.404media.co/researchers-secretly-ran-a-massive-unauthorized-ai-persuasion-experiment-on-reddit-users/), April 2025).

**Liar's dividend**
- Across five survey experiments with more than 15,000 US adults, politicians who falsely called a real scandal "fake news" or a deepfake gained support, but mainly for scandals reported in text; against video evidence the tactic mostly failed ([Schiff, Schiff and Bueno, *American Political Science Review*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/liars-dividend-can-politicians-claim-misinformation-to-evade-accountability/687FEE54DBD7ED0C96D72B26606AA073), February 2024).

## Existing coverage
- **Fraud:** illegal everywhere. Enforcement is weak against the scale of losses, and the FBI's own report points to foreign scam centers as a target of a recent executive order ([FBI IC3 2025 Annual Report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)).
- **AI voices in robocalls:** the FCC ruled unanimously on 8 February 2024 that AI-generated voices count as "artificial" under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, making such robocalls illegal without consent ([FCC](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-makes-ai-generated-voices-robocalls-illegal)). It fined the consultant behind the fake Biden robocalls in New Hampshire $6 million in September 2024 ([FCC](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-issues-6m-fine-nh-robocalls)). Enforced.
- **Non-consensual intimate deepfakes:** the federal TAKE IT DOWN Act, signed 19 May 2025, makes publishing them a crime and requires platforms to remove them within 48 hours of a victim's request, with platform duties enforced by the FTC from 19 May 2026 ([Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAKE_IT_DOWN_Act), secondary). A first conviction was reported in April 2026 (secondary, unverified against court records).
- **Election deepfakes:** 33 states have enacted laws, per Public Citizen's tracker updated 5 October 2026. Hawaii's is marked as struck down ([Public Citizen](https://www.citizen.org/article/tracker-legislation-on-deepfakes-in-elections/)). Most are untested.
- **EU AI Act Article 50:** from 2 August 2026, AI providers must mark synthetic audio, images, video and text in a machine-readable way, and people who publish deepfakes must disclose them ([Article 50 text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)). Parts of the article have since been amended, and enforcement is too new to judge.
- **AI persuasion:** no law specifically limits AI systems designed to change people's political views.

## Triage
1. **Covered by law and enforced?** **Partly, with weak enforcement.** Fraud, AI robocalls and intimate deepfakes are illegal, and there has been some real enforcement (the $6 million FCC fine). But reported fraud losses keep rising by about a quarter a year, most state election laws are untested, and the EU labelling rules have only just started.
2. **An action both sides accept?** **Yes, narrowly.** Both sides support enforcing fraud law and teaching people simple verification habits, such as calling back on a known number before moving money. The skeptics object to treating election misinformation mainly as an AI problem, not to fighting fraud.
3. *(Skipped, because step 1 applies.)*

**Result: Enforcement problem** for fraud and impersonation: the laws exist, the gap is investigation, cross-border cooperation and platform compliance. **Communicate** on elections and trust, to correct the common belief that deepfakes have already swung elections.

**Communication note:** Tell people the honest shape of it: the measured damage so far is theft, not stolen elections. The practical defense is personal and cheap: treat any urgent request for money by voice or video as suspect, and confirm through a separate channel. On elections, say that warnings were louder than effects in 2024, and that overstated fear of fakes can do the liar's work for them.

## Related scenarios
- Opposite: "Better shared understanding and forecasting."
- Combines with: "Concentrated power or AI-enabled surveillance" (whoever controls AI assistants shapes what people see), "Over-reliance, lost skills and unhealthy AI companions" (persuasion through trusted AI companions).
- Leads to: weaker public ability to respond to every other scenario on the map, if trust in shared facts erodes.
